Q1. What are the publicly available Alzheimer's datasets for researchers?

Several publicly available datasets are available for Alzheimer's disease research. These include the Alzheimer's Disease Sequencing Project (ADSP) data, the National Alzheimer's Coordinating Center's (NACC) Uniform Data Set (UDS), and the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) data. Additionally, the AD Knowledge Portal, the NIAGADS Data Sharing Service, and the AD Data Initiative offer access to various datasets.

Detailed resources for accessing Alzheimer's data:

Alzheimer's Disease Sequencing Project (ADSP): This project provides data like genotyping and sequencing data from individuals with Alzheimer's disease and related dementias through NIH-approved data repositories, with biospecimens also available.

National Alzheimer's Coordinating Center (NACC): NACC's Uniform Data Set (UDS) is a standardized, longitudinal dataset collected via annual visits to Alzheimer's Disease Research Centers (ADRCs). It includes demographics, family history, medical information, cognitive assessments, and clinical data.

Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI): ADNI collects and analyzes data from participants with and without Alzheimer's disease, including MRI, PET, and CSF data.

AD Knowledge Portal: This portal provides access to multi-omic data from human samples, cell-based and animal models, analysis results, and research tools from NIA-supported Alzheimer's programs.

NIAGADS Data Sharing Service: NIAGADS is a national genetics repository that facilitates access to genotypic data for Alzheimer's genetics research.

AD Data Initiative: ADDI houses datasets and tools to enhance data sharing for Alzheimer's and related dementias research.

AD Discovery Portal: The AD Discovery Portal, part of the AD Workbench, is a user-friendly catalog of Alzheimer's datasets.

Global Alzheimer's Association Interactive Network (GAAIN): GAAIN offers access to clinical, genetic, and imaging data related to Alzheimer's disease.

Dementias Platform UK (DPUK): DPUK provides access to over 60 datasets through their Data Portal.


Q2. What are the publicly available mild cognitive impairment datasets for researchers?

Several publicly available datasets are available for researchers studying Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI), including the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) dataset, the National Alzheimer's Coordinating Center (NACC) database, and the National Centralized Repository for Alzheimer's Disease and Related Dementias (NCRAD). These resources offer a wealth of data, including clinical, imaging, genetic, and biomarker information, to support research into MCI and its progression.

Here's a breakdown of the prominent datasets:

Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI): ADNI is a comprehensive, longitudinal study that tracks individuals with MCI, Alzheimer's disease, and healthy controls over time. ADNI provides a rich collection of data, including:

Clinical data: Demographic information, cognitive assessments (e.g., MMSE, MoCA), and medical history.

Imaging data: MRI scans (structural and functional) and PET scans.

Genetic data: Information on genetic markers associated with Alzheimer's disease.

Biomarker data: Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and blood-based biomarkers.


2. National Alzheimer's Coordinating Center (NACC): NACC is a central repository that collects and standardizes data from Alzheimer's Disease Research Centers (ADRCs) across the United States. NACC's database includes:

Clinical data: Detailed information on participants with AD, MCI, and healthy controls.

Neuropathological data: Information on brain tissue samples from deceased participants.


3. National Centralized Repository for Alzheimer's Disease and Related Dementias (NCRAD): NCRAD provides access to biological samples (DNA, RNA, CSF, etc.) collected from individuals participating in NIA-funded studies, including those with MCI and AD.

4. Other datasets:

Health and Retirement Study (HRS): HRS includes cognition data as part of its public data and sensitive data products.

MultiConAD: MultiConAD is a multilingual dataset for Alzheimer's disease and MCI research, incorporating data from multiple languages and assessment types.

AD Discovery Portal: The AD Discovery Portal, part of the AD Workbench, is a user-friendly catalog of Alzheimer's datasets.

Dem@Care: Dem@Care offers data from lab and home-based experiments, including video, audio, and physiological sensor data.

OpenNeuro: OpenNeuro hosts various neuroimaging datasets, including some relevant to MCI and AD.

NIAGADS: The National Institute on Aging [Link: NIAGADS https://dss.niagads.org/datasets/] provides access to genetic datasets related to Alzheimer's disease and related dementias.

These datasets offer valuable resources for researchers to investigate the causes, progression, and potential interventions for MCI, contributing to the broader effort to understand and combat Alzheimer's disease.
